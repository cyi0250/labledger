# EXP-0001 — Insertion sort against sorted() on short lists

## Question

On short lists (2 to 64 items), is a pure-Python insertion sort faster than the built-in `sorted()`?

## Setup

- [`bench.py`](attachments/EXP-0001/bench.py) sorts one shuffled list of each length (`random.seed(0)`) with each
  function, and takes the median of 7 timing runs of 2,000 sorts each (`timeit.repeat`).
- CPython 3.11.8 on Linux, AMD Ryzen 9 5950X, with nothing else running.

## Commands

```bash
cd experiments/attachments/EXP-0001
python3 bench.py > timings.csv
```

## Results

From [`timings.csv`](attachments/EXP-0001/timings.csv): microseconds per sort of one list, median of 7 runs. The ratio
is insertion sort ÷ `sorted()`, so above 1 means `sorted()` is faster.

| Length | Insertion sort [µs] | `sorted()` [µs] | Ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.187 | 0.078 | 2.40 |
| 4 | 0.353 | 0.089 | 3.98 |
| 8 | 1.203 | 0.118 | 10.19 |
| 16 | 3.016 | 0.187 | 16.09 |
| 32 | 12.606 | 0.367 | 34.38 |
| 64 | 49.214 | 0.906 | 54.35 |

## Reading

`sorted()` is faster at every length measured: 2.4 times at 2 items, 54 times at 64. A likely reason, not measured
here: `sorted()` runs in C, while the insertion sort interprets every comparison and move in Python.
